# Shami — syllabus v0.3

A 100-lesson course in **Damascene Arabic** (الشامي), in the Assimil manner, for English-speaking learners who
already read Arabic script. Explanations in English.

*Titles, story beats and lesson order will still move once the dialogues are written. Arabic titles are drafts
to be checked against the Syrian sources.*

## Shape

| Piece | What |
|---|---|
| Lessons 1–100 | Dialogue → notes → new words → practice, like Tsheh-tû. Each grammar point is met in the dialogue first, then explained. |
| Every 7th lesson (7, 14 … 98) | Revision: gathers the grammar of the previous six into tables, plus one longer dialogue. |
| Bridge lessons B1–B14 | **Outside the count**, one after each revision: lessons 1–6, revision 7, bridge B1, then lesson 8, and so on. The same sentences in Shami and MSA side by side: what changes and why. |
| Passive phase 1–49 | Read, listen, understand. |
| Active phase from 50 | Second wave, as in Assimil: each lesson also revisits lesson *n − 49*, produced from English. |
| Levels | 1–35 ≈ A1 · 36–70 ≈ A2 · 71–100 ≈ B1 (with B2 recognition items). |
| Bridge size | A mini-lesson: a short MSA dialogue retelling the week's scene, the week's key Shami sentences with their MSA version, and contrast notes. About half a normal lesson. |

## Pacing

Modelled on the Assimil rhythm, starting at its week-2 size since learners already read Arabic.

| Week | Lessons | Dialogue lines | Arabic words | New words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–7 | 4–5 | ~15 | 5–6 |
| 2 | 8–14 | 5–6 | ~20 | 8 |
| 3 | 15–21 | 6–7 | ~28 | 10 |
| 4 | 22–28 | 7–8 | ~35 | 12 |
| 5 | 29–35 | 8–9 | ~45 | 14 |
| 6–7 | 36–49 | 10–11 | 55–65 | 14–16 |
| 8–10 | 50–70 | 11–13 | 80–100 | 15–18 |
| 11–12 | 71–84 | 14–16 | 110–140 | 18–20 |
| 13–14 | 85–98 | 16–18 | 150–180 | 20 |

Grammar order: present tense first (dialect speech needs it); derived verb forms early, one roughly every
week: Form II (30), V (37), III (44), VIII (46), VII (53), X (59), VI (72).

## Conventions (decided)

**Transcription: Lingualism's system, with dots instead of hooks.**

| Sound | Written | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ع | `3` | 3andi, sā3a |
| ء and ق (said as a glottal stop) | `ʔ` | ʔahwe, ʔāl |
| ح | `ḥ` | ḥilu |
| ص ض ط ظ | `ṣ ḍ ṭ ẓ` | ṣaḥn, ḍaww, ṭayyib, ẓarīf |
| ج | `j` (as in French *jour*) | jdīd |
| ش خ غ | `š x ɣ` | šū, xubz, šiɣl |
| long vowels | `ā ē ī ō ū` | bēt, yōm |
| short vowels | `a i u` only (no ə) | biddi, kint, kill |
| ة | `-e`; `-a` after emphatics and throat consonants | madrase, sā3a |
| stress | acute accent only where the rules don't predict it | kátabu |

**Arabic script**
- ق is kept where Damascus says ʔ: قهوة, قال.
- ث ذ ظ are spelled as pronounced: تلاتة, هادا, كتير. The MSA spelling appears in the bridge lessons.
- Partial vowel marks, as in Lingualism (sukūn on clusters, vowels where ambiguous), with a show/hide toggle in the app.

## The story

**Adam** (آدم), mid-twenties, grew up in Paris. His father **Nabīl** left
Damascus for France before Adam was born. Adam understands the Arabic of his parents' kitchen, answers in
French, and studied MSA at university, so when he does speak he sounds "like a newsreader". He comes to
Damascus for the first time, to learn to speak properly and to meet the family he only knows from video calls.

| Who | |
|---|---|
| **Tēta** | Adam's grandmother. Lives in the old courtyard house near Bāb Tūma, where Adam sleeps in his father's old room. Stories, proverbs, corrects everyone's Arabic. |
| **Abu Sāmi** and **Umm Sāmi** | Adam's uncle (his father's brother) and aunt. Modern flat in Mezze. Hospitality, cooking, memories. |
| **Sāmi** | Cousin, late twenties, works in IT. Taxis, cafés, work talk. |
| **Līna** | Cousin, student. Shopping, gossip, wondering whether to leave. |

**Two homes:** the old house (fountain, jasmine, souks on foot) and the Mezze flat (cars, offices, everyday
modern life). Scenes move between them.

**Tone: realistic and warm.** Family comedy and tenderness in front; real life around it. Prices in the souk
from the first shopping lesson; from A2 onward, electricity cuts, relatives abroad, a friend displaced from
Aleppo, Tēta's memories of the war years, Līna's question of staying or leaving. Adam's father returning to
Damascus for the first time in decades (lesson 46) is the emotional centre of the course.

Sources: the grammar follows Mark W. Cowell, *A Reference Grammar of Syrian Arabic* (1964); column **C** gives
the chapter of Cowell where each point is described. Range of situations after Haki Kill Yoom; everyday topics and Syrian usage checks from Syrian Arabic
Voices and the Syrian speakers of the two Shwayy 3an Ḥāli books.
